MATERIALS & DEVICES · SEP 2024 – PRESENT

MICROFABRICATION & PHOTONICS

Femtosecond Laser Writing in Amino Acid Crystals

First Author · Manuscript Submitted

This project investigates how thermally sensitive L-threonine single crystals can be processed into optical devices. Femtosecond laser writing creates surface microstructures and internal waveguides while limiting damage to the surrounding crystal.

My research combines crystal preparation and microfabrication with optical device testing. The study connects processing conditions with modification quality, light guiding and polarization response, and examines device dissolution in an aqueous environment.

Research methods and implementation
  • Processing study: vary pulse energy, scanning speed and focal depth to identify conditions for continuous internal modification without excessive lateral damage.
  • Microstructure assessment: examine surface patterns and buried tracks using microscopy, and quantify track continuity, dimensions and positioning accuracy.
  • Device fabrication: form internal optical waveguides using a written cladding around an unmodified crystal core.
  • Optical evaluation: characterise transmission, propagation loss and polarization selectivity, alongside the crystal's second harmonic response and aqueous dissolution.

ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING & BIOMATERIALS

Electric Field Assisted 3D Printing of Peptide Structures

Research Contributor

I participated in the development of an electric field assisted digital light processing (DLP) printing system and short peptide bioinks. The platform combines ultraviolet projection, parallel electrodes and a programmable rotating build platform to control both printed geometry and the orientation of peptide assemblies.

My involvement includes short peptide synthesis and printing system development. The project uses electric field alignment, light curing and rotation between layers to produce Bouligand architectures, in which the alignment direction turns progressively through the structure.

Research methods and implementation
  • Material preparation: combine self assembling short peptides with a photocurable hydrogel formulation so that aligned assemblies can be fixed during exposure.
  • Platform operation: coordinate vertical motion and platform rotation with patterned light exposure and electric field alignment.
  • Structural control: vary the rotation between layers to produce different helical arrangements without changing the overall part geometry.
  • Project validation: assess alignment using atomic force microscopy and image orientation analysis, and compare compression tests with finite element predictions of stress distribution.

FUNCTIONAL MATERIALS & BIOELECTRONICS

Amino Acid Crystal Piezoelectric Sensors

Research Contributor

This project studies the relationship between amino acid crystal structure and piezoelectric response, and integrates L-threonine crystals into flexible sensors. A crystal sensing layer is combined with conductive electrodes and polymer films to convert mechanical deformation into electrical signals.

I participated in sensor fabrication and device testing, with hands-on experience in subcutaneous implantation in mice. The wider study evaluates mechanical response, repeated loading, material degradation and physiological signal monitoring.

Research methods and implementation
  • Material study: compare crystal packing, mechanical properties and piezoelectric response to select a sensing material.
  • Device construction: integrate the crystal layer with polyaniline electrodes and polylactic acid films in a flexible layered structure.
  • Bench testing: record voltage under controlled loading, assess response consistency during repeated cycles, and evaluate signals during joint movement.
  • Biological evaluation: the project includes mouse implantation studies of tissue response and separate demonstrations of respiration and limb movement sensing in rats.
